Connecting and Communicating Together (CCT)

A locally shaped project with global possibilities

Connecting and Communicating Together is a flexible project developed by Anderson Therapies that brings children and young people together to explore communication, connection and belonging.

CCT can be developed with schools, colleges, health services, charities, community organisations and other partners in the UK and internationally.

Each project is co-designed with local partners and shaped by the children and young people who take part. This allows CCT to reflect the strengths, priorities, cultures and different ways of communicating within each setting, rather than applying the same programme everywhere.

The project also creates opportunities to learn alongside practitioners, families and communities, contributing to the ongoing development of the Communication for Connection Framework.

Why this matters

Communication is shaped by relationships, identity, belonging, culture and the environments we share.

The project begins by exploring what matters most to children and young people. From there, participants become curious about the role communication might play in helping them connect with others and move towards what matters to them.

As children and young people learn alongside one another, they discover shared ways of connecting, learn from different strengths and perspectives, and experience communication as something created together.

Together, new possibilities emerge that are often difficult to create alone.

What the project involves

CCT is delivered through a set of group workshops that bring children and young people together to explore, experiment and reflect.

The workshops create opportunities for children and young people to:

  • explore what communication means to them
  • discover what helps them connect with others
  • experiment with different ways of communicating, such as the use of language, creativity, movement and shared activities
  • reflect on their experiences together
  • develop a shared understanding of communication and connection within their community

Each project is developed with local partners and adapted to the children, young people and community involved.

Where CCT is developing

CCT in Ghana

A CCT pilot is being developed in Accra with Talking Tips Africa Foundation and local partners, bringing together professional knowledge, cultural understanding and the experiences of Ghanaian children and young people. The project will be co-designed for the local context. Discussions are also emerging in The Gambia, Jamaica and London, including with charities, primary schools, special schools and further education settings.

Who can partner with CCT?

CCT can be adapted for organisations that support children and young people, including:

  • primary and secondary schools
  • special schools
  • colleges and further education settings
  • community groups and youth organisations
  • charities and non-governmental organisations
  • health and mental health services
  • universities and professional training organisations
  • local and national government partners

The project does not rely on formal diagnoses, assessments or identified communication needs. Instead, it brings together children and young people with different communication styles, strengths and experiences to learn with and from one another.

Hopes, benefits and anticipated outcomes

CCT creates opportunities for children and young people to:

  • better understand themselves and one another
  • build greater awareness of different communication styles, strengths and perspectives
  • develop more flexible ways of communicating and connecting with others
  • explore what matters to them and what might help them move towards it
  • contribute their ideas to more inclusive and connected communities
  • develop as peer ambassadors, with opportunities for some young people to support or co-facilitate future workshops

For the community around them, CCT aims to create opportunities to:

  • listen to and learn from the experiences of children and young people
  • develop a shared understanding of communication, connection and belonging
  • strengthen relationships between children, families, practitioners and organisations
  • identify practical ideas that can continue beyond the workshops
  • share learning with the wider community through resources, reflection and co-facilitator training

Learning across communities

Every CCT project creates opportunities to learn locally and across communities.

Learning from each project contributes to a growing understanding of communication, connection and belonging across different settings and cultures. It also helps shape the ongoing development of the Communication for Connection Framework.

Working in partnership

CCT is not a fixed programme introduced into a community. Every project is developed alongside local partners who understand their community, culture and context.

Partnership opportunities may include:

  • Host partner: coordinating the project locally and supporting the participation of children and young people.
  • Co-facilitator partner: taking part in training and supporting delivery, reflection and shared learning throughout the project.
  • Advisory partner: contributing local knowledge, cultural insight, professional expertise or lived experience.
  • University or evaluation partner: helping to explore, evaluate and share what is learned through the project.

Funding

CCT is not a commercial service sold directly to children, young people or their families.

Projects are developed through partnerships, commissioning arrangements, grants or other appropriate funding agreed with host organisations. The aim is to remove financial barriers to participation wherever possible.
